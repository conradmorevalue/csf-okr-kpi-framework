# CSF / OKR / KPI Framework

## Core Hierarchy (always apply this order)

Treat the three as a single system, never in isolation:

1. **CSFs (Critical Success Factors)** — the foundation  
   What must be true / the must-win areas for the organization (or team) to succeed.  
   These are the 3–7 highest-leverage areas where success or failure is decided.  
   CSFs answer: “What is truly critical to win?”

2. **OKRs (Objectives and Key Results)** — the goals  
   Bold, time-bound objectives and measurable key results that move the CSFs forward.  
   OKRs answer: “What ambitious outcomes will we drive this period to achieve the CSFs?”

3. **KPIs (Key Performance Indicators)** — the health metrics  
   Ongoing, usually lagging or leading indicators that show whether the business (or the OKRs) is healthy and on track.  
   KPIs answer: “Are we progressing and staying healthy day-to-day?”

**Mental model**:  
CSFs = What’s critical to succeed  
OKRs = The goals you set to achieve it  
KPIs = The metrics that show progress

## Definitions (use these exact framings)

- **CSFs**: Identify the must-win areas. Focus resources where success depends most. Form the foundation of strategy.
- **OKRs**: Define bold goals. Stretch teams with measurable outcomes. Align everyone around what matters most.
- **KPIs**: Track ongoing performance. Show if you’re on or off track. Keep the business healthy day to day.

## How They Work Together (procedural rules)

- Start every strategy or planning conversation with CSFs. Without clear CSFs, OKRs and KPIs create activity without focus (“growth without focus is just noise”).
- Derive OKRs directly from CSFs. Every Objective should clearly advance one or more CSFs.
- Select KPIs that either (a) measure progress toward the Key Results or (b) monitor the ongoing health of the CSFs.
- Limit CSFs to a small number (typically 3–7 for a company, fewer for a team). More than that dilutes focus.
- Review CSFs less frequently (annual or when strategy changes). Reset OKRs quarterly. Monitor KPIs continuously (weekly/monthly).
- Make the linkage explicit: every KPI should map back to an OKR or CSF; every OKR should map back to a CSF.

## Best Practices

- Begin each quarter by identifying (or reconfirming) the one priority / CSF that truly matters most right now.
- Invite the team into CSF and OKR conversations before locking metrics. Shared ownership increases commitment.
- Explicitly decide what will NOT be prioritized. Strong strategy is as much about exclusion as inclusion.
- When reviewing performance, always ask: “Is this KPI/OKR moving the underlying CSF?”
- Avoid the common trap of treating OKRs as stretch KPIs or KPIs as goals. Keep the roles distinct.
- For long-term success the order of importance is usually: clear CSFs > well-chosen OKRs > good KPIs. Metrics without critical focus produce disciplined confusion.

## Step-by-Step Application

When a user wants to define or improve their system:

1. Clarify context (company stage, industry, team size, current strategy or goals).
2. Surface or propose 3–7 CSFs. Test each with: “If we fail here, can we still succeed overall?”
3. For each priority CSF, draft 1–3 Objectives (qualitative, inspirational, time-bound).
4. For each Objective, define 2–4 Key Results (quantitative, measurable, ambitious but achievable).
5. Identify the small set of KPIs that will indicate health and progress (prefer leading indicators where possible).
6. Map everything: CSF → OKR(s) → KPI(s). Show the cascade clearly.
7. Recommend review cadences and ownership.

## Examples

**SaaS / Client Success**  
- CSF: Client retention & expansion  
- OKR: Cut time-to-value (onboarding to first value) by 30% this quarter  
- KPIs: Time-to-value, Net Revenue Retention, logo churn, expansion revenue rate

**Product-led Growth**  
- CSF: Activation & habit formation  
- OKR: Increase % of new users who complete the core action loop within 7 days from 22% → 40%  
- KPIs: Activation rate, D7/D30 retention, feature adoption of core loop, time-to-aha

**Sales-led B2B**  
- CSF: Pipeline quality and conversion  
- OKR: Raise SQL-to-Closed-Won rate from 18% to 28% while holding average deal size  
- KPIs: Win rate, sales cycle length, pipeline coverage ratio, stage conversion rates

**Operations / Delivery**  
- CSF: Reliable, high-quality delivery at scale  
- OKR: Reduce critical defects escaping to production by 50% and keep on-time delivery ≥ 95%  
- KPIs: Escape defect rate, on-time delivery %, mean time to recovery, customer-reported issues

When generating examples, always show the three layers linked, never isolated lists.

## Wheel of Strategy — 20 Essential Questions for CEOs

70% of CEOs say their strategy is clear. Only 10% of their teams agree. That gap is where misalignment lives, priorities get lost, and momentum dies. Real alignment does not come from louder messaging — it comes from sharper thinking.

Use the Wheel of Strategy to force clarity across four areas. Answer these 20 questions with the team (leadership meeting, quarterly reset, or offsite). If the team cannot answer them confidently and consistently, go back to the strategy table.

### 🧭 Purpose & Direction
1. Why do we exist? Who actually needs us?
2. What’s our mission in one clear sentence?
3. What do we believe that drives how we operate?
4. Where do we want to be in 3 years?
5. What would success look like if nothing held us back?

### 📊 Market & Advantage
6. Who is our highest-value customer?
7. What pain are they feeling every day?
8. What’s changing in our industry? How do we stay ahead?
9. Why do people choose us—or not?
10. What can we offer that’s hard to copy?

### 📈 Goals & Metrics
11. What are our top 3 priorities right now?
12. What does success look like this quarter?
13. What’s the one number that matters most today?
14. How do we review progress each week?
15. What milestone will tell us we’re winning?

### ⚙️ Actions & Tactics
16. What must we deliver in the next 90 days?
17. Who owns each outcome? By when?
18. What’s currently blocked? How do we fix it fast?
19. What quick wins will build momentum now?
20. When and how will we check in and adjust?

**How this connects to the CSF / OKR / KPI system**  
- Purpose & Direction + Market & Advantage → feed the CSFs (what is truly critical to win).  
- Goals & Metrics → directly shape the OKRs and the selection of KPIs.  
- Actions & Tactics → turn the OKRs into owned, time-bound execution with weekly review.  

Use the 20 questions as a diagnostic filter before (or while) defining CSFs, OKRs, and KPIs. If the answers are vague or inconsistent across the team, the hierarchy will be built on sand.
