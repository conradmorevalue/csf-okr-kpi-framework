# Diagnostics & Common Pitfalls

## Quick Diagnostic Questions

When reviewing an existing system, ask:

1. Can the leadership team list the 3–7 CSFs without looking them up?
2. Does every current OKR clearly advance at least one CSF?
3. Are there KPIs that do not map to any CSF or OKR? (If yes → candidates for removal or demotion)
4. Are OKRs being used as “stretch KPIs” (i.e., just harder versions of the same metrics)?
5. Is the team optimizing a KPI that actually moves the wrong CSF?
6. How many CSFs exist? (>7 is almost always a red flag)
7. When was the last time CSFs were explicitly revisited?

## Common Failure Modes

- **KPI theater**: Lots of dashboards, no clear CSFs → activity without strategic progress.
- **OKR inflation**: Too many Objectives or Key Results → diluted focus and “everything is a priority.”
- **Missing foundation**: Strong OKRs and KPIs but no agreed CSFs → teams pull in different directions.
- **Lagging-only metrics**: Only tracking lagging KPIs (revenue, churn) without leading indicators that predict CSF health.
- **Static CSFs**: Treating CSFs as permanent when strategy or market has shifted.
- **Isolated tools**: Different teams using different frameworks without a shared CSF layer.

## Red Flags in Language

- “Our OKRs are basically our KPIs with targets.”
- “We have 15 critical success factors.”
- “We track 40 KPIs but aren’t sure which ones matter most.”
- “We set OKRs every quarter but never talk about what is truly critical.”

## Recommended Interventions

- Run a 60–90 min CSF workshop: “If we could only win in 5 areas this year, which ones?”
- Force a one-page cascade: CSF → top OKRs → top KPIs.
- Introduce a “kill list”: metrics and initiatives that no longer serve a CSF.
- Add a standing agenda item in leadership reviews: “CSF health check.”
